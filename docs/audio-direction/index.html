Coin Duel Sound Direction

Every sound cue for the board game, each with the chosen sound, its contenders side by side and why. Press a button to listen; nothing plays until you do. 73 cues (68 required, 3 extra, 2 optional loops), 94 chosen files and 146 contenders, all CC0.

“Sound effects are in scope. The feel has have a grit and viscerality to it, while staying readable and not too exagerated.”

Owner’s direction, 2026-10-24

“Any artistic decision will show a quick mock in a page, as well as justification for the use. If there are several contenders, all should be represented and justified.”

Owner’s rule for artistic decisions

How to listen

  • Use headphones or real speakers: several heavy hits sit below 150 Hz.
  • Play plays the chosen sound; numbered buttons pick one variation. In the game each play also gets a small random pitch and level change, which the scenes below apply.
  • Letters A and B are the contenders, each with the reason it is not the default.
  • The sliders copy the proposed in-game split: master, effects and ambience, each saved per player.
  • Levels on this page already include each cue’s gain from cues.json, so cues compare as they would in the game.
  • The page plays the MP3 copies; the game ships the same sounds as Ogg Vorbis with MP3 as the Safari fallback.

Every choice here is a proposal awaiting the owner’s pick. Swapping a contender in is a one-line change in the recipe.

The palette

Gritty comes from real recordings of real objects; readable comes from giving each kind of event one material and one shape, and keeping every sound short.

Game thingMaterialShape
Units (select, move, death)A real coin on varnished woodTap, slide, spin-down
HitsStruck metal (anvil, plates, blades)Light = bright and short; heavy = anvil with a body punch
Blocked by armour or ForcefieldBlade pingHigh, thin, fast decay
Hit on a defending unitIron plate thudLow, dull, no ring
Swings and shotsAir (sticks, bamboo, bow)No metal, so the hit carries the material
InterfaceStone piece, clay chip, card, map, bookDry, under 0.6 s
Turn signalsBronze bell (you), war drum (enemy), horn (first contact)One strike each
AbilitiesOne physical gesture each: bowl, gong, chain, clockwork, cauldron, clothNever a synth riser or zap

Rules that keep it readable

  • No cue is longer than 2.4 s except the three result stingers and the loops.
  • Heavy impacts duck the interface by 6 dB and the ambience by 4 dB for about half a second.
  • At most 16 voices, with per-cue caps (move steps 3, heavy hits 2, big abilities 1).
  • Repeated cues get ±0.3 to 0.6 semitones and ±1 to 1.5 dB per play, so they do not machine-gun.
  • Peaks sit at about −3 dBFS; the gain in cues.json sets each cue’s place in the mix.
  • References: Into the Breach for one clear sound per event; Darkest Dungeon and Battle Brothers for the dry, physical grit.

Hear it in context

Short scripted moments using the chosen sounds, the cue gains, the per-play jitter and the ducking rule.

  • Select a Berserker, three steps, heavy swing, heavy hit, the Footman dies, end turn.

  • The same light swing three times: into a Paladin's armour, into a defending Soldier, into a bare Mercenary.

  • A blind shot at a square with a hidden Trip Mine, then a second shot that kills.

  • Your bell, Smite on a Royal Wizard with splash on two neighbours, the Wizard falls, the Siege counter ticks.

  • A Rogue hides, the enemy steps adjacent, Assault strikes and reveals the Rogue.

  • Three march waves into the Market ring, the Market is captured, then contested.

  • A Footman falls, Queso's prompt rings, the coin rises again.
