  1. C1Meaning of "adjacent", and melee reachQ7
    Positions
    RL: adjacent = 8 squares (Chebyshev), melee = 4. BA, PX: adjacent and melee = 8. AB: everything 4, behind a switch.
    Decision
    Manhattan distance 1 (4 squares) for "adjacent", melee reach, "1 square away" and "range 1". Chebyshev only for "(including corners)" and for drawn shapes. Switch ADJACENCY.
    Why
    One metric for movement, LOV, range and adjacency (L34, L37). L45 has to add "(including corners)", so the default excludes corners. A LOV-1 unit then always sees every square adjacent to it, and no unit is "adjacent but unable to hit". The only text that needs 8 neighbours (Slam's "5 opposite adjacent squares", L110) is a drawn shape and gets an explicit template (8.12). The synthesis brief directs Manhattan.
  2. C2AP per turnSuperseded by R1Q15 Q16 Q17 Q19
    Positions
    RL, AB: 10. PX: 8. BA: 12 fixed, as round(0.06 x budget); first player 6 on turn 1.
    Decision
    12 fixed; the first player's first turn 6; no carry-over.
    Why
    The brief says to adopt the balance analyst's default. 12 lets 22-30-unit armies act with about a third of themselves per turn (BA 4.2), divides evenly by AP costs 1-4, and scales with the budget.

Balance findings

The headline numbers from the balance analysis (docs/analysis/balance.md), with the suggestion that addresses each one.

Direction only

These figures come from a simple attrition model: armies start in contact and trade attacks. It has no movement, range or kiting, terrain, fog or Defend, and none of the abilities a player activates, so it undervalues area damage, support units and the Ranger's kiting.

It also ran before your rulings: fixed AP pools (6 to 30, with 12 as the default) instead of the 10-to-20 curve of R1, no cap or a 30- or 40-unit cap instead of the 50 of R2, armies that mix both factions (R4 now keeps each army to its own 7 units), a 1/1 commander instead of the toughness 5 of R8, and 8-square adjacency where our default is 4. Re-run it under the current rules (S05) before changing unit stats.

Your stance (R5): you noted the swarm dominance and expect real players not to use swarms that effectively, so balance gets iterated after playtests.

Headline

  • AP saturation. With a fixed pool, an army deals full damage until it has fewer units left than the pool can activate, so cheap, tough bodies dominate: 100 Soldiers won 88-100% of matchups at every AP pool from 6 to unlimited, and the 50 and 100 tiers lose to almost everything on raw stats.
  • Pace. Seven of the 14 units have Movement 1 and every step costs AP: at 12 AP, moving a 30-unit mixed army forward 6 rows costs 179 AP, about 15 turns.
  • Defender's advantage. Stepping into contact uses the unit's one action, so the enemy strikes first; in the model the side that struck first won every mirror.
