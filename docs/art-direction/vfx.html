Coin Duel · art direction · VFX and motion

Effects & motion

The owner's rule asks for a mock of every artistic decision with all contenders shown and justified. This page plays one event script in the three contender effect styles and in the recommended hybrid, side by side, on the adopted board (B3 sand slabs, T2 coins with palette D faces). Below it: the recommended effect library, the signal colours, the UI motion language and the animation queue. The full specification is docs/analysis/research-vfx.md.

"Sound effects are in scope. The feel has have a grit and viscerality to it, while staying readable and not too exagerated."

Owner, 2026-10-24

Recommended: "Struck metal, inked"

Style A's restrained physical impacts on metal coins (a 60-90 ms hit-stop, a jolt and recoil, metal chips and sparks, sand puffs, scuffs, a capped shake up to 6 px) carry the weight. Style B's readability devices (keylined numerals, breakdown chips, tile-exact outlines, direction marks, plaques) carry the explanation. Style C survives only as capped accents (additive sparks, one short light shaft for holy abilities, a smoke puff on mine blasts).

Decision 1

Three contender styles on the same script

Each board plays the same events: a two-step move, a Berserker killing a Rogue (8 damage), a Ranger shooting a Paladin (3 − 2 armour = 1), a Smite on a Juliomancer with 3 splash on its neighbours (a Mercenary dies), and an Artificer stepping on a hidden mine (5). The single buttons replay one beat on all four boards at once.

A · "Struck metal"

Base of the recommendation

Restrained physical impacts: wind-up, lunge, a hit-stop of 60-90 ms, the target jolts and recoils, its rim flashes, metal chips and sparks fall on the sand, a scuff stays. Deaths shatter into wedge shards. References: Battle Brothers (pieces lunge, recoil, consequences stay on the field), Darkest Dungeon (the held beat and meaty foley, without its zoom), Sakurai's "Hitstop", Eiserloh's trauma shake.

Pros

  • Weight and grit without exaggeration
  • Fits metal coins and the toy diorama
  • Cheap: no post-processing

Cons

  • Doesn't say why (armour? Defend?)
  • No splash shape, no "Mine!" or "Blocked!"
  • Chips are faint at full-board zoom

B · "Ink and impact"

Devices adopted, look rejected

Flat, inked 2D shapes on the board: burst stars, impact rings, slash strokes, speed lines, stamped numerals with breakdown chips, inked area outlines, plaques. Tokens don't move; deaths are an ink X. References: Into the Breach (tile-anchored overlays, the board never hidden), Zach Gage's "Three Reads", Mark of the Ninja (invisible information drawn as rings).

Pros

  • Most readable: what, how much, why
  • Shape-coded; direction explicit
  • Reduced motion is trivial

Cons

  • No weight: reads as UI, not a blow
  • Slides into comic exaggeration
  • Flat stamps clash with the lit diorama

C · "Ember and smoke"

Rejected (accents only)

Particle-forward: real bloom (UnrealBloomPass), additive glows, embers drifting after hits, smoke plumes, light flashes. References: Diablo III/IV, Hades, Divinity: Original Sin 2, Hearthstone (shake scaled by cost, inside an exaggerated package).

Pros

  • Spectacle and atmosphere
  • Magic reads as magic

Cons

  • Bloom washes out Argent, Or and the sand
  • Smoke lingers into the next action
  • Needs post-processing; colour-led
  • The opposite of "not too exaggerated"

Recommended · "Struck metal, inked"

Recommended

A's physics for the weight, B's devices for the explanation: keylined numerals in the signal colours with breakdown chips ("−2" armour), tile-exact outlines for the Smite plus and the mine blast, an arrow trail for the shot, plaques for "Mine!". Two C accents: additive sparks and a 150 ms light shaft for Smite.

Why

  • Weighted score 95/100 vs A 82, B 75, C 42 (research-vfx §2.4)
  • Every effect: shape + colour + keyline

Watch

  • Keep one strong effect per beat
  • Shards and chips stay within 0.6 tile
The recommended style in detail

Effect library

Each button sets up a small scene and plays one engine event (or a composite of events) as specified in research-vfx §6. The line under the board names the event and the sound cue id. The recommended board shows you (brass bezels) at the bottom and the opponent (iron bezels, milled outline) at the top; some hidden-information effects are shown from the Lojrar player's side and say so. The mock board is zoomed in (about 95 px per tile against about 36 px at full-board zoom in the game), while numerals and plaques are drawn at their in-game pixel sizes, so they look smaller here than they will in play.

Decision 2

Signal colours: "Mint & forge"

Effect colours that stay clear of the seven grade colours of palette D, each with its shape code and an INK keyline (the colours alone reach only 1.0-2.0:1 on sand). Decisions 6 and 7

Animation queue: beats, settle points, "settle, then send"

One batch: your Berserker charges two squares and kills an enemy Mercenary next to your Paladin, under Kulega (Fine Print heals the Paladin 1). Beats play in engine order; the next beat may start at the previous beat's settle point (the diamond); tails overlap. Confirming a new intent early snaps to the last settle point and sends at once. The interruption plaque stays for its full hold even when skipped.
