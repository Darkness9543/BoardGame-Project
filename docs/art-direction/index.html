Coin Duel Art Direction

Every artistic decision for the browser board game, each with a quick mock, all of its contenders side by side and a justified default. “Coin Duel” is a working title. Models, icons, colours and numbers are the real assets and the researcher’s computed values; where a mock is only an approximation (the S2 parchment panel, the B1 and B2 patches, the F2 parchment), its card says so.

Owner ruling since the research (2026-10-24): two factions with one unit per tier. The Empire: Footman (1), Soldier (2), Berserker (5), Priestess (10), Paladin (20), Royal Wizard (50), Celestial Emissary (100). Lojrar Clan: Mercenary (1), Ranger (2), Rogue (5), Juliomancer (10), Artificer (20), Beast of Knorund (50), Scourge of the Gods (100). Section 4 is laid out by faction accordingly, and under palette D the spec’s “units of white” are tier 1 (Argent).

How to read this page

  • One section per decision. Each opens with a Recommended card: the proposed default, why, and how confident the researcher is.
  • Then every contender appears side by side with its own mock and a short pros/cons list. Tags: Recommended Contender Option (a setting, not the default) Rejected / Reference (kept so the comparison is complete).
  • IDs such as S1, T2, OW-1, K1b, M2, F3-lite or P1 match research-art.md, so feedback can name them directly.
  • Interactive parts: the vision-mode tabs in section 3, symbol swaps in section 4, and three 3D viewers (sections 2, 5 and 6) that load the real model files. Everything else is static, and each viewer shows the static render until it is ready.

Every default on this page is a proposal awaiting the owner’s choice. Nothing is final until the owner picks.

Section 1

Overall style

What “stylized” (L47) means for the 3D world and for the interface.

Recommended (proposal)

S1 for the 3D world, with S2’s parchment and ink language in the UI only

The KayKit and Kenney kits are free (CC0), coherent with each other, and both cameras were tested with the real files; ownership recolouring is cheap because KayKit ships four colour variants and a Kenney colormap swap recolours a whole kit. S2 would need original painted art for buildings and tokens plus NPR shaders so 3D objects do not look pasted on. Its parchment-and-ink language is therefore kept for panels, fonts and menus, where it costs little.

Researcher's confidence: Medium

S1"Toy diorama" low-poly

Recommended

Look: chunky low-poly 3D with gradient-atlas or flat shading, soft short shadows, saturated accents on neutral ground. Assets: KayKit Medieval Hexagon and Kenney Castle / Fantasy Town / Tower Defense / Nature kits, all CC0. References: The Battle of Polytopia, Dorfromantik, Bad North.

Pros

  • Free, coherent, fast
  • Both cameras work (tested with the real files)
  • Ownership recolouring is easy (KayKit colour variants, Kenney colormap swaps)

Cons

  • Generic indie look ("asset-pack" risk)
  • Textures must stay quiet so the tokens pop

Contender

  • Matches the parchment UI

  • More production: painted buildings and tokens are original art
  • Texture noise threatens legibility
  • Integrating 3D objects needs NPR shaders; otherwise they look pasted on

Section 2

Token design and owner cue

The spec makes every unit a token whose colour is its grade and whose symbol is the unit (L44). This section covers the token form (decision 2), how you tell your tokens from the opponent’s (decision 6) and the state overlays.

Recommended (proposal)

T2 3D coin carrying the exact T1 face

It gives the same pixels as T1 in the default top view and a physical coin in iso. The spec requires 3D objectives (L46) and an iso toggle (L48); next to them a flat sticker looks wrong in iso, while T2 keeps a lit edge and gets a natural lift when selected. The cost stays trivial for fewer than 100 tokens, though small sizes need MSAA.

Researcher's confidence: Medium-high

Owner

T1Flat disc with icon (printed chit / decal)

Contender

A textured quad or SVG. Layers, outside in: soft drop shadow (offset 0.03, 25 % black) → owner outline → bezel ring 0.07 wide (owner tone plus faction ornament) → face (grade colour, radial gradient highlight → base → shadow at 35 %/30 %) → hairline ring (ink at 35 %) → icon box 0.62 × face diameter → numeral cartouche → status badges. Unlit or flat.

Pros

  • Cheapest; trivial palette swaps
  • Crisp and identical to the 2D design in top view

Cons

  • In iso it looks like a sticker: the icon is foreshortened to 0.58× vertically and there is no thickness
  • Selection and lift need fake shadow tricks

T23D coin cylinder with embossed rim

Recommended

CylinderGeometry(r, r, h, 64) with r from the size ladder and h from the thickness ladder, a raised rim torus (tube 0.025–0.03), and the same T1 face on the top as a CanvasTexture (256² is enough). The side is reeded for metal grades and plain enamel for colour grades. Bezel/side: brass #C9A23A (metalness 0.75, roughness 0.35) for you, blackened iron #2E2A27 (0.6/0.55) for the opponent; face metalness ≤ 0.2 so highlights do not wash out the grade colour.

Pros

  • Looks the same as T1 in top view (verified in objectives-v2-top)
  • Reads as a real coin in iso: shadow, rim and a lit edge keep its identity
  • Selection is natural: lift by 0.15 and the shadow grows
  • Matches the 3D objectives (L46) and the iso toggle (L48)

Cons

  • Needs MSAA at small sizes
  • Slightly more work than T1, though still trivial for fewer than 100 tokens
  • The icon is still foreshortened in iso

Owner cue: your tokens vs the opponent’s

Recommended (proposal)

OW-1 light/dark bezel plus OW-3 smooth/milled outline

The grade owns the face colour, so ownership needs another channel, and it must still work in mirror matches (Q11). A light vs dark bezel is lightness-based, so it survives every colour-vision deficiency and never competes with a grade hue; the smooth vs milled outline adds a shape cue that still reads in greyscale. The cost is that the outline can no longer carry the euro-edge grade cue (E).

Researcher's confidence: Medium · Faction identity then sits inside the bezel as an ornament: 24 pearls for the Empire, 3 rune notches and a knot band for the Lojrar

Each card shows a tier-10 pair at 64 px (Empire Priestess vs Lojrar Juliomancer, the two tier-10 units under the owner ruling) and a mixed 6×3 board at about in-game size.

Section 3

Grade palette and non-colour grade cues

Seven grades follow the euro distribution 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 (L5) and the token colour is the grade (L44). Roughly 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have a red-green deficiency, so every palette is shown as a colour-blind player would see it.

Recommended (proposal)

Palette D “Heraldic euro” by default, palette C as the “Clarity mode” toggle

D has the best normal-vision separation of all palettes (minimum ΔE00 24.8) and stays at 13.2 or above under every simulated dichromacy, against 4.4 for the euro-literal A2 under deuteranopia. Heraldry has exactly two metals and five colours, so the seven tinctures cover the seven grades and follow the euro (Argent = €1 silver centre, Or = €2 gold, Gules = €10 red, Azure = €20 blue, Vert = €100 green), and its Petra Sancta hatching is a built-in, on-theme non-colour cue. C is the most colour-blind-robust palette (no pair below 17.9), so it is offered as an accessibility toggle rather than the default.

Researcher's confidence: Medium · Rendering rule from heraldry: metals (Argent, Or) render as polished metal, the five colours as enamel inlays; dark ink on metals, light ink on colours, except Vert, which keeps dark ink (light ink reaches only about 2.9:1)

Normal vision: the palette hexes as specified.

Each coin: radial gradient highlight → base → shadow, the unit symbol and the grade numeral in the palette’s icon ink (whichever of #1B1410 / #FFF8E7 has the higher WCAG contrast). Simulation: Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009) at severity 1.0 (full dichromacy, the worst case); greyscale is Rec. 709 relative luminance. Base colours are the simulated hexes from palettes.json; the highlight, shadow and ink stops were run through the same simulation for this page. Thresholds for small tokens: ≥ 20 distinct at a glance, 12–20 side by side, < 10 confusable.

Non-colour grade cues (decision 4)

Recommended (proposal)

Numeral cartouche + subtle size ladder always, Petra Sancta hatching with palette D, thickness ladder in iso

Colour alone cannot carry seven grades for everyone, so the grade also gets cues that survive any colour deficiency. Euro coins print their value, so the numeral needs no learning, and the size ladder mirrors real coin sizes; hatching comes free with palette D. Rank pips and euro-edge outlines stay available as settings, and Clarity mode switches to palette C with pips on.

Palette D, Empire symbols, one cue at a time and then the recommended combination.

Section 4

Unit symbols

The symbol on top of the token represents the unit (L44). Every symbol below is the real game-icons.net SVG, inlined and recoloured; click an alternate to preview it on the coin.

Recommended (proposal)

game-icons.net primaries from the Lorc / Delapouite family

Each primary was checked at 22 px, the icon size on a 25-row board at 1080p (about 36 px per tile). The two units that share a tier colour, one per faction, have clearly different silhouettes: spears vs X, helm vs archer, axe vs hood, chalice vs eye, shield vs gear, hat vs horned head, wings vs skull. 36 of the 39 primaries come from Lorc or Delapouite, the two authors with matching stroke weight, and meaning comes from the unit’s ability where possible (Line Formation becomes a phalanx of spears, Mind’s Eye a third eye).

Researcher's confidence: Medium-high · No reuse across roles: lorc/crossed-swords is the Mercenary, so the UI Power stat uses lorc/pointy-sword

Owner ruling: one unit per tier per faction, so the grid is two faction groups by tier. Coins use palette D, the faction bezel ornament (Empire pearls, Lojrar runes), the numeral cartouche and the D hatching; the 22 px copy is the in-game token size at 1080p.

Neutral objectives: the Keep and the Market

Each objective occupies 2×2, is captured by a unit in range 1 including corners (L45), and is “represented by 3d objects and a symbol in the middle” (L46). All contenders were rendered from the real files.

Recommended (proposal)

Keep: K1b, the KayKit castle squashed to Y×0.45

It reads as a castle from both cameras and its four colour variants make ownership obvious. Squashed it stays about 1.5 tiles tall, inside the 1.6-tile height budget, so in true isometric it hides about 2 rows behind it instead of about 5 for the unsquashed K1.

It fits the footprint at about 1.06 tiles tall, is very readable in iso (striped awning, produce crates) and shares the castle’s atlas and colour variants. M2 is a strong contender; its stalls would need a colormap recolour to show ownership.

A square grid (L13), 25×25 with the corners cut off with a radius of 4 (L30), blockers possible, and a top view by default with an isometric toggle (L48).

Recommended (proposal)

Orthographic top view by default; orthographic true isometric as the toggle

Section 7

Fog of war

Each player only sees what their units see (L17). LOV is a Manhattan diamond: LOV 1 shows the 4 orthogonal squares, LOV 2 shows 12 squares around the unit (L37), so fog edges are diamond-shaped staircases.

Recommended (proposal)

F3-lite: tile-exact vision boundary with an ink edge

The rules depend on exactly which tiles are seen: Rogue stealth only activates “if not inside the LOV of another enemy creature”, so the player must be able to read the LOV boundary tile by tile. F3-lite draws that boundary as a crisp ink line along the Manhattan diamond while dimming and desaturating the fogged tiles; F1’s feather turns the diamond into a blob and F2 hides terrain detail. Hatching stays optional because it makes the board busier.

Researcher's confidence: Medium-high

Each mock: a 9×9 patch of B3 slabs with your Paladin (LOV 2) in the centre. The 12 squares of its diamond are in view, including a Lojrar Rogue two squares ahead; the rock and the Keep corner are in fog but still drawn, as terrain and objectives always are.

F3-liteTile-exact boundary with an ink edge

Recommended

Fogged tiles: greyscale at ×0.8 brightness, dimmed like F1; optional diagonal hatch (#1E1914 at 55 %, 1.2 px lines every 5 px). Boundary: a crisp 2 px ink line (#2B1D0E) along the exact LOV boundary.

Pros

  • Shows exactly which tiles are seen
  • The rules depend on it: Rogue stealth needs "not inside the LOV of another enemy creature"

Cons

  • Busier with hatching on
  • Can look "technical"

F1Dim + desaturate

Contender

Fogged tiles: saturation ×0.15, brightness ×0.45. Boundary: feathered, at most 1/3 tile (a blurred per-tile mask).

Pros

  • Familiar RTS look

Cons

  • Diamond edges blur into blobs, so the exact LOV becomes ambiguous

F2Parchment “terra incognita”

Contender

Fogged tiles: parchment (parchmentBasic.png at 92 %, approximated here with an SVG noise filter) plus a 25 % sepia overlay; blockers and objectives redrawn on top as ink. Boundary: a soft torn-paper edge (feather 0.5 tile plus noise).

Pros

  • The most thematic, especially with S2

Cons

  • Hides terrain detail (blockers and objectives must be inked on top)
  • The visible area needs stronger contrast than this quick mock has

The researcher’s full-board mocks and the two corner-cut readings

Both images show the same 25×25 board, units and LOV layout under F1, F2 and F3; they differ only in how “corners cut off with a radius of 4” is read. Manhattan cut removes the cells with x + y < 4 at each corner (10 cells per corner, an octagon): the default reading, because LOV is measured in Manhattan steps. Euclid cut rounds the corners like CSS border-radius: 4 and removes only 3 cells per corner. A third reading, a concave quarter-disc bite of 13 cells per corner, is drawn in section 6. In these mocks the blur of F1 and F2 was not yet clipped to the board mask, which is why production must clip every overlay to it.

What is drawn under fog (Q10, Q15)

  • Terrain, blockers and objectives, including their ownership, are always drawn.
  • Enemy units and mines are never drawn in fog. The server should not even send them, which also prevents cheating.
  • Your own stealthed units and mines appear translucent with a dashed bezel (section 2, state overlays).
  • Hovering a unit previews its LOV diamond as an outline.

Implementation hint for both cameras: a 25×25 single-channel visibility texture sampled in every material by world XZ (three.js onBeforeCompile). Nearest filtering gives F3, bilinear gives F1, and tall objects in fog darken correctly in iso without volumetric fog.

Factions

Two clans or factions for now, more possible later (L43). Faction colours live in UI chrome, banners, building tints and the commander crest, never on token faces, which belong to the grade palette.

Recommended (proposal)

Empire E1 “Imperial crimson & gold” and Lojrar L1 “Northern clan: wolf & iron”

E1’s Roman/Byzantine crimson and gold with the eagle is the classic imperial look, and L1’s Norse wolf, pine and iron fits a clan whose beast is “of Knorund”. KayKit’s four building colours map onto two factions × two colourways (Empire red with blue as the mirror alternate, Lojrar green with yellow), so mirror matches are covered. Quaternius licence conflict

The Quaternius pack pages say “CC0 License”, but the site-wide quaternius.com/license.html now says “Quaternius Asset License (QAL) v1.0”, “Last updated: 8/28/2026”. QAL forbids redistributing the raw assets, which is a risk if the game repository is public. The conflict is unresolved, so Quaternius stays a backup only; prefer Kenney or KayKit.

Open art questions for the owner

Research section 2 (Q1–Q17), condensed. Each has a working default so implementation can start; Q1–Q3 are settled by the owner ruling.

#QuestionDefault or answerConfidence
Q1Which faction owns the 14 listed units?Resolved by the owner ruling: one unit per tier per faction (Empire: Footman, Soldier, Berserker, Priestess, Paladin, Royal Wizard, Celestial Emissary; Lojrar Clan: Mercenary, Ranger, Rogue, Juliomancer, Artificer, Beast of Knorund, Scourge of the Gods).Resolved
Q2What are the "2 variations" of each coin value?Answered by the same ruling: the two units of a tier are one per faction. They share the tier colour, so their silhouettes must differ (4).Resolved
Q3"Units of white" (Baba Yaga).Resolved: with palette D, tier 1 is Argent (white). Rules should still name tiers by number, with colour as presentation.Resolved
Q4Does "Size 2" mean a 2×2 footprint?Default yes: one coin of diameter 1.84 tiles centred on the block (Beast of Knorund).Medium
Q5Corner cut shape ("cut off with a radius of 4").Default (a) Manhattan triangle, 10 cells per corner (6).Low
Q6Do blockers block vision, or only movement?Default movement only; fog is a pure union of LOV diamonds.Low
Q7Does the token colour change when a commander changes a unit's grade (Sabrina)?Default yes: show the effective grade plus a small commander-effect pip.Medium
Q8Commander token appearance; are the six commanders split by faction?Default ivory face #F3EAD2, faction crest on the bezel, portrait icon, chess-king badge; commanders faction-agnostic.Low
